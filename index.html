01 /THE PRINCIPLE

A reason to do the work

A good call is made long before the countdown.

Launch control appeals to me because it is where engineering meets responsibility to a crew and a team. The final decision should rest on systems that were tested honestly, procedures that were rehearsed, and people who can speak up when something is wrong.

I am learning that discipline at the test stand: writing the controls, understanding the hardware, watching the data, and staying calm when reality departs from the plan. I want to earn trust through preparation and sound judgment.

01 / GROUND CONTROL

SOFTWARE + OPERATIONS

Make the system legible when the stakes rise.

I wrote ground-control software for ERPL waterflow and liquid-rocket static-fire tests. It brings pressure, temperature, load, valve state, and system health into one operating view while supporting remote actuation and automated sequences. I integrated the data-acquisition hardware and served as ground controller on more than eight tests.

THE LESSON An operator needs clear state, useful alarms, and a path to safe action.

02 / PROPULSION

DESIGN + ANALYSIS

Turn a stalled engine into a buildable one.

As project lead on an ERPL regeneratively cooled engine, I rebuilt a Python workflow connecting performance, heat transfer, injector sizing, and material strength. It let the team compare more than 1,000 design iterations and move the project into fabrication. The engine has not yet fired.

03 / THE TEAM

LEADERSHIP + READINESS

The work is bigger than one person.

As president of ERPL, I helped lead a 180-member organization through design reviews, test readiness, operations, and the day-to-day work of building hardware. The standard I care about is whether the team can understand the system, challenge a decision, and carry the work forward together.

03 /FIELD EXPERIENCE

Theory meets the thing that actually happens.

In professional test engineering, I have worked where mechanical systems, instrumentation, software, and field operations meet—from heavy industrial equipment to autonomous maritime systems. The details belong to those employers; the transferable habit is to test assumptions in hardware and follow evidence when a design does not behave as expected.

That is the kind of work I want to keep doing in launch and vehicle test organizations.
